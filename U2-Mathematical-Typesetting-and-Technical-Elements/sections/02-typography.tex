	\subsection{Superscripts and subscripts}
		Superscripts and subscripts are commonly used to represent exponents (such as $3^2$, $x^{-3}$) and indexed quantities (such as $m_1$, $K_0$) respectively. In \LaTeX, superscripts are typeset using the \emph{caret} symbol (\lstinline|^|) while subscripts are typeset using the \emph{underscore} symbol (\lstinline|_|).
		
		If a superscript or subscript contains more than a single character, the entire exponent or index must be enclosed in braces \lstinline|{ }|.

	\subsection{Fractions and roots}
		In \LaTeX, fractions are typeset using the \lstinline|\frac| macro. The general syntax is
\begin{lstlisting}
	\frac{numerator}{denominator}
\end{lstlisting}
		For fractions contained inline, where vertical space is limited, standard slash notation \lstinline|a/b| or the \lstinline|\tfrac{num}{den}| macro can be used.

		\LaTeX\ allows us to typeset the square root using the \lstinline|\sqrt| macro which has the general syntax as
\begin{lstlisting}
	\sqrt{expression}
\end{lstlisting}

	\subsection{Pre-defined functions and operators}
		Standard mathematical functions (such as trigonometric, logarithmic and limit operators) must not be typed as plain text in math mode; otherwise, \LaTeX\ would render them in variable italics. For example, \lstinline|sin(x)| typesets as a product of $s$, $i$ and $n$. Hence to render this correctly in upright roman typeface with appropriate surrounding whitespace, \LaTeX\ provides pre-defined operator macros for a bunch of functions as given below.
		\begin{itemize}
			\item \emph{Trigonometric and hyperbolic functions}: \lstinline|\sin|, \lstinline|\cos|, \lstinline|\tan|, \lstinline|\arcsin|, \lstinline|\arccos|, \lstinline|\arctan|,\\ \lstinline|\sinh|, \lstinline|\cosh|, \lstinline|\tanh|
			
			\item \emph{Logarithmic and exponential functions}: \lstinline|\exp|, \lstinline|\log|, \lstinline|\ln|
			
			\item \emph{Limits and optimization}: \lstinline|\lim|, \lstinline|\min|, \lstinline|\max|, \lstinline|\sup|, \lstinline|\inf|
			
			\item \emph{Linear algebra}: \lstinline|\det|, \lstinline|\ker|, \lstinline|\deg|
		\end{itemize}
	
	\subsection{Summation, product and integral operators}
		In \LaTeX, the summation, product and integral operators are typeset using the \lstinline|\sum|, \lstinline|\prod| and \lstinline|\int| macros. These are variable-sized operators and get rendered in different sizes, depending on whether they are in the inline or display modes.
		
		The following code typesets these in the inline mode:
\begin{lstlisting}
$\sum_{i=1}^{N}(2i-1)$, $\prod_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2^k}$ and $\int_{0}^{5}e^{-x}dx$
\end{lstlisting}
		which gives the following output:
		\lstoutputraw{
		$\sum_{i=1}^{N}(2i-1)$, $\prod_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2^k}$ and $\int_{0}^{5}e^{-x}dx$
		}
		
		In display math mode, variable-sized operators render larger than in inline mode, and their limits adjust automatically. The following code typesets the above expressions in the display mode:
\begin{lstlisting}
$$\sum_{i=1}^{N}(2i-1)$$ $$\prod_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2^k}$$ $$\int_{0}^{5}e^{-x}dx$$
\end{lstlisting}
		which gives the following output:
		\lstoutputraw{
			$$\sum_{i=1}^{N}(2i-1)$$ $$\prod_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2^k}$$ $$\int_{0}^{5}e^{-x}dx$$
		}
	
	\subsection{Greek letters}
		In physics, letters from the Greek alphabet are widely used to represent various physical quantities. In \LaTeX, lowercase Greek letters are typeset by prefixing the letter's English name with a backslash, as given in the following table:

			\centering
			\begin{tabular}{|cc|cc|cc|cc|}
				\hline
				\textbf{Letter} & \textbf{Macro} & \textbf{Letter} & \textbf{Macro} & \textbf{Letter} & \textbf{Macro} & \textbf{Letter} & \textbf{Macro} \\
				\hline
				{$\alpha$} & {\lstinline|\alpha|} & {$\eta$} & {\lstinline|\eta|} & {$\nu$} & {\lstinline|\nu|} & {$\upsilon$} & {\lstinline|\upsilon|} \\
				{$\beta$} & {\lstinline|\beta|} & {$\theta$} & {\lstinline|\theta|} & {$\xi$} & {\lstinline|\xi|} & {$\phi$} & {\lstinline|\phi|} \\
				{$\gamma$} & {\lstinline|\gamma|} & {$\iota$} & {\lstinline|\iota|} & {$\pi$} & {\lstinline|\pi|} & {$\chi$} & {\lstinline|\chi|} \\
				{$\delta$} & {\lstinline|\delta|} & {$\kappa$} & {\lstinline|\kappa|} & {$\rho$} & {\lstinline|\rho|} & {$\psi$} & {\lstinline|\psi|} \\
				{$\epsilon$} & {\lstinline|\epsilon|} & {$\lambda$} & {\lstinline|\lambda|} & {$\sigma$} & {\lstinline|\sigma|} & {$\omega$} & {\lstinline|\omega|} \\

		For the uppercase Greek letters that do not resemble any of the letters from the English alphabet, the typesetting is done in \LaTeX\ by capitalizing the first letter of the macro for the same lowercase letter. For instance, when the macro for the lowercase lambda $\lambda$, which is \lstinline|\lambda|, is capitalized as \lstinline|\Lambda|, we obtain the uppercase lambda $\Lambda$.
